I implemented `ledger.py` to the spec, expanded `test_ledger.py`, and committed both as `578fe3d` on `main`. All 27 tests pass across repeated runs with `python3 -m unittest`.

**Design**
- Each `Ledger` has one `threading.Lock` that guards every transfer and snapshot. Each operation is therefore atomic, and opposite transfers cannot deadlock because only one lock exists.
- Balances and amounts must be built-in `int` (`type(x) is int`), so booleans are rejected. Balances must be in `[0, MAX_BALANCE]` and amounts in `[1, MAX_BALANCE]`.
- The amount is validated before any account lookup, so a bad amount with a missing account raises `ValueError`.
- Failed transfers leave every balance unchanged. A same-account transfer returns `True` iff funds suffice and never mutates.
- The constructor copies its input, and `snapshot()` returns a fresh dict.

**Assumptions to check**
- A non-string account name (for example `None`, `1`, or a list) raises `KeyError`, the same as an unknown account. The spec does not say what to do here.
- The concurrency tests use `join(timeout=30)` only as a deadlock guard. They assert no timing.
- The commit uses the repo's configured identity (`mengsig`). I did not add the `Co-Authored-By` line from the harness reminder, because the repo's CLAUDE.md forbids Claude attribution in commits.
